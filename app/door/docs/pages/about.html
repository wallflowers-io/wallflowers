What WallFlowers is

WallFlowers is the digital interior for your online presence: messaging, forums and events, embedded into your existing website, or linked from your Instagram page. You can visit a site, or join as a member.

A community, and its members

  • A community is what a person makes at wallflowers.io/signup: its rooms, its events, its resources, its members, and its public page at wallflowers.io/<address>, where the address is the short name chosen at signup (such as harbour-lane; no angle brackets). Whoever makes it owns it; admins help run it.
  • A member signs in with a passkey (no password) and, in WallFlowers' app or on a site built on it, talks in its rooms, goes to its events, reads its resources and lists things on its board.
  • Who sees what. Rooms and what is said in them are for the community's members. Its public page (its look, its events, its resources) is for anyone.
  • Your data. WallFlowers doesn't sell or share it. End-to-end encryption is coming soon. The protocol is open, and WallFlowers is one operator of it.

Each change is one named operation (an op, such as forum.post) on one object, and the model lists them all. Your app reaches only this community, its rooms and its page, and what your app itself made: never the member's other communities.

What you can build

Working code to copy, each two static files with no build step:

  • the starter: sign in, rooms, posting;
  • members and events: upcoming events with Going or Not going, and the members (index.html, app.js). Until the community is published, it lists only the events its own session made.{{EXAMPLE_OWNERS}}
  • a resource library: the community's resources for anyone; a member writes and publishes with the Resources editor (index.html, app.js);
  • a listings board: list, browse and take down (index.html, app.js).

Members and events and the resource library read the public page by the community's address: set CONFIG.address, since a session can't read it. Each app.js names the port to serve it on.

Built on WallFlowers: egregores-echoes.com

The website of Egregore's Echoes, an art installation: a Next.js site on its own server. Its community (the members, their rooms and what they say) lives on WallFlowers, so the site holds no accounts, no passwords and no member data. It signs people in with WallFlowers, and reads and writes the community as the signed-in member.
